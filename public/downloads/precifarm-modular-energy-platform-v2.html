The five-minute version

  1. One module. 2.56 kWh LiFePO₄ at 51.2 V / 50 Ah TARGET — the only storage building block Precifarm designs.
  2. Energy and power scale separately. Adding modules adds kWh. Adding kW requires a larger Power Core. Conflating the two is the most common residential-storage sizing error (§26).
  3. Three physical forms. P1 Go carries ~1 kWh. P2 Home rolls and grows from 1 to 4 modules. Pod installs and scales from 2 modules to tens of kWh.
  4. One software layer. Energy OS owns monitoring, prediction, diagnostics, PAYGO and service — the defensible layer. Cells, power stages and connectors are bought.
  5. Kenya-first. 230 V / 50 Hz, ~4.7 peak-sun-hour worst-month design basis, KSh 20–28/kWh grid cost, 10 kW domestic net-metering cap, M-Pesa settlement, and a service model built to repair a module, not replace a system.

01THE ENERGY PLATFORM

1.1 Design principle

Energy storage should be a positive experience — simple to use, easy to move, easy to expand, and ready to connect to solar, home and electric mobility.

1.2 Platform definition

Precifarm designs one energy platform that takes different physical forms as the customer's needs grow. The platform is defined by four invariants:

3.3 The two derivations that matter

Energy.

E = V × Ah
E = 51.2 V × 50 Ah
E = 2,560 Wh
E = 2.56 kWh                                            [CALC — confirms TARGET]

Maximum continuous DC power.

P = V × I
P = 51.2 V × 50 A
P = 2,560 W
P = 2.56 kW                                             [CALC]

ERN-02: v1.0 stated this figure as "≈ 2.5 kW". The arithmetic result is 2.56 kW. The discrepancy is immaterial to design but must not propagate into data sheets, where 2.56 kW nominal / de-rated values shall be quoted explicitly.

C-rates.

C_discharge = I / Ah = 50 A / 50 Ah = 1.00 C            [CALC]
C_charge    = I / Ah = 30 A / 50 Ah = 0.60 C            [CALC]

A 1.0 C continuous discharge on prismatic LFP is achievable but is thermally the binding constraint in Kenyan ambient conditions (§11). Cell selection must be qualified at 1.0 C and 40 °C ambient, not only at the 0.2 C / 25 °C conditions typical of published datasheets VALIDATE.

5.2 ERN-01 — the v1.0 inverter/module mismatch

Finding. v1.0 specified a P2 base SKU with a 4 kW continuous inverter and one 2.56 kWh module.

DC current required for 4 kW AC output:

I_DC = P_AC / (η_inv × V_nom)
I_DC = 4,000 W / (0.94 × 51.2 V)
I_DC = 4,000 / 48.13
I_DC = 83.1 A                                                      [CALC]

C-rate demanded of a single module:
C = 83.1 A / 50 Ah = 1.66 C                                        [CALC]

Module continuous limit = 1.00 C (50 A)                            [TARGET]

Conclusion. A single Energy Module cannot support a 4 kW continuous AC output. v1.0 is internally inconsistent.

Resolution — two-tier SKU definition [PROPOSED]:

The P2-4.8 Power Core may be installed with one module, but firmware shall limit continuous output to 2.4 kW until a second module is enumerated. This is an Energy OS rule, not a hardware change — capability follows installed capacity.

06POWER CORE

6.1 Principle

The Power Core contains everything that converts, controls and protects energy. The Energy Module contains only energy. This separation is the platform's most important architectural decision.

External validation [BENCHMARK]: Tesla's Powerwall 3 Expansion unit is explicitly battery-only and relies on the leader unit's inverter, allowing capacity to scale to 54 kWh across one inverter. BYD's Battery-Box towers similarly require an external or top-mounted control unit and a compatible third-party inverter.

7.2 ERN-03 — the 51.2 V parallel scaling limit

Finding. v1.0 described a "Pod Array" of 10–51+ kWh while retaining a 51.2 V nominal architecture. At 51.2 V, capacity can only grow by paralleling modules, and current grows linearly.

Parallel LV architecture at 51.2 V nominal:

N modules → I_max = N × 50 A,  P_max = N × 2.56 kW

N = 4  → 200 A    → 10.24 kW      practical
N = 8  → 400 A    → 20.48 kW      busbar/fuse cost rising steeply
N = 20 → 1,000 A  → 51.20 kW      NOT ACCEPTABLE for this product class   [CALC]

A ~1,000 A DC bus in a residential or SME cabinet implies very large conductors, expensive high-breaking-capacity fusing, significant I²R loss and a difficult arc-flash case. Neither benchmark competitor does this. BYD scales the same 2.56 kWh module in series to 512 V while holding output current at 25 A per tower BENCHMARK.

Resolution — dual architecture [PROPOSED], decision E-03 (§25):

ArchitectureApplicationTopologyDC voltageCurrent at maxLabel
LV-48P1, P2, Pod ≤ 4 modulesParallel modules51.2 V nominal≤ 200 APROPOSED
HV-SPod ≥ 5 modulesSeries string, optional parallel strings256 – 512 V nominal≤ 100 APROPOSED

HV-S worked example:

10 modules in series:
V = 10 × 51.2 V = 512 V
Ah = 50 Ah (unchanged in series)
E = 512 V × 50 Ah = 25,600 Wh = 25.6 kWh
I_max = 50 A → P = 512 × 50 = 25.6 kW                              [CALC]

51.2 kWh via 2 parallel strings of 10 series:
V = 512 V,  Ah = 100 Ah,  E = 51.2 kWh,  I_max = 100 A             [CALC]

Current falls from 1,000 A to 100 A for the same energy and power — a 10× reduction in conductor and protection duty.

Consequence: HV-S crosses into a higher electrical-safety class. Isolation, creepage/clearance, insulation monitoring, arc protection and installer competency all change. CERT — this must be scoped with a Kenyan certification consultant before Pod pilot (§24, P3 gate).

ERN-08 addendum: v1.0's "≥ 500 V DC isolation, module-to-chassis" is adequate for LV-48 but insufficient for a 512 V string. HV-S modules require isolation rated for the full string voltage plus margin, not the module voltage.

11.2 Heat generation

Module ohmic loss at maximum continuous discharge:

P_loss = I² × R_internal
Assume R_internal(module, 16s pack, incl. busbars) = 12 mΩ        [ASSUMPTION — VALIDATE]

P_loss = (50 A)² × 0.012 Ω
P_loss = 2,500 × 0.012
P_loss = 30 W per module at 1.0 C                                 [CALC]

As fraction of delivered DC power: 30 / 2,560 = 1.17 %            [CALC]
Power Core loss at rated output (P2-4.8):

P_loss = P_out × (1/η − 1)
η = 0.94                                                          [ASSUMPTION]
P_loss = 4,800 × (1/0.94 − 1) = 4,800 × 0.0638 = 306 W            [CALC]

Engineering consequence: the Power Core, not the battery, is the dominant heat source at rated output. Forced-air cooling should be applied to the Power Core first; modules can remain passive in P2 [VALIDATE by thermal test].

22.2 The ethical and legal constraint

Remote lockout technology is standard practice in African PAYGO solar, and Kenyan law does not currently contain a statute specifically regulating it BENCHMARK. Academic and policy analysis recommends that remote lockout be limited but not prohibited, precisely because unrestricted lockout can remove access to light, water or refrigeration BENCHMARK. The industry's voluntary GOGLA Consumer Protection Code sets minimum consumer-protection standards BENCHMARK.

Design implication: Precifarm should not build a hard remote kill switch for a product that may be a household's only source of light or refrigeration.

26POWER VERSUS ENERGY

This section exists because conflating kWh and kW is the single most common cause of mis-sold residential storage.

26.1 The analogy

        ENERGY  =  TANK SIZE  (kWh)          how much you have
        POWER   =  PIPE SIZE  (kW)           how fast you can use it

A bigger tank does not make water come out faster. Adding Energy Modules does not increase AC output power.

26.3 In Precifarm terms

MORE MODULES        →  MORE ENERGY  (kWh)  →  longer runtime
LARGER POWER CORE   →  MORE POWER   (kW)   →  more simultaneous load

27.3 What these equations deliberately omit

Real system design must additionally consider: cell temperature history and calendar ageing; actual depth-of-discharge distribution; inverter efficiency curve versus load (not a single figure); battery ageing over the warranty term; reserve policy interaction with outage statistics; inverter surge and motor-start behaviour; solar variability and soiling rate; the customer's real load profile rather than a daily total; site electrical constraints; and statutory export limits.

No sizing tool should present a single-number answer without a sensitivity range.
